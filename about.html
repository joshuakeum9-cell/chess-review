Game Review is a free chess analysis tool I built to do what premium game review products do: take a finished game, grade every single move, show you exactly where it went wrong, and teach you the move you should have played instead. It runs entirely in your browser, costs nothing, needs no account, and never uploads your games anywhere.

Why I built this

The fastest way to improve at chess is not playing more games. It is understanding the games you already played: which moves threw away the win, which tactic you walked past, which "safe" move quietly handed over the position. The tools that do this well sit behind subscriptions, and the free tiers ration how many games you can review.

I wanted that feedback loop to be free for anyone who is trying to get better, and I wanted to find out how much of a premium review experience one person could build from first principles. So I built the whole pipeline myself: the chess rules engine, the move classification logic, the accuracy model, and the coaching explanations, with Stockfish 17 doing the raw calculation in a pool of web workers on your own machine.

What it does

A verdict on every move

Brilliant, Great, Best, Excellent, Good, Book, Inaccuracy, Mistake, Miss, and Blunder, with the badge popping right on the board square.

Accuracy and game report

An accuracy score for each player, an estimated game rating, phase-by-phase breakdowns, and the key moments that decided the game.

Coaching, not jargon

Every flagged move gets a plain-English explanation grounded in the position: what hangs, what the punishing reply is, and the line that was better.

Deep verification

Any move flagged as an error is re-checked at greater depth before you see the verdict, so false accusations are rare by design.

Explore freely

Drag pieces to test your own ideas and ask the engine, draw arrows and highlights to think out loud, flip the board to either point of view.

Fast and private

A full game reviews in seconds. The engine runs in your browser, so your games never leave your computer.

Built from scratch, measured against the best

This project is also a piece of engineering I am proud of. The move generator is validated with perft against the standard reference positions. The classifier was benchmarked ply-by-ply against Lichess's published judgments across thousands of positions, and the accuracy model tracks the numbers the major sites report to within a couple of points on real games. When something disagreed, I chased down which side was right and kept the receipts in the repository's benchmark suite.

Engine: Stockfish 17.1 (GPL). Pieces: Cburnett set (CC BY-SA). Openings: the Lichess ECO dataset, 3,810 named lines. Everything else: written by hand.
