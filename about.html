Why it exists

Improvement is a feedback loop, and the loop was paywalled

The fastest way to improve at chess is not playing more games. It is understanding the games you already played: which moves threw away the win, which tactic you walked past, which safe-looking move quietly handed over the position. The tools that do this well sit behind subscriptions, and the free tiers ration how many games you can review.

I wanted that feedback loop to be free for anyone who is trying to get better, and I wanted to find out how much of a premium review experience one person could build from first principles. So I built the whole pipeline myself: the chess rules engine, the move classification logic, the accuracy model, and the coaching explanations, with Stockfish 17 doing the raw calculation in a pool of web workers on your own machine.

The rules it was built under

  • Free forever, for every game, at full depth
  • No account, no upload, no tracking of any kind
  • Verdicts benchmarked against the major sites, not guessed
  • Explanations in plain English, grounded in the position

What you get

A full premium-style review, free

A verdict on every move

Brilliant, Great, Best, Excellent, Good, Book, Inaccuracy, Mistake, Miss, and Blunder, with the badge popping right on the board square.

Accuracy and game report

An accuracy score for each player, an estimated game rating, phase-by-phase breakdowns, and the key moments that decided the game.

Coaching, not jargon

Every flagged move gets a plain-English explanation grounded in the position: what hangs, what the punishing reply is, and the line that was better.

Deep verification

Any move flagged as an error is re-checked at greater depth before you see the verdict, so false accusations are rare by design.

Explore freely

Drag pieces to test your own ideas and ask the engine, draw arrows and highlights to think out loud, flip the board to either point of view.

Fast and private

A full game reviews in seconds thanks to a pool of parallel engine workers. Everything runs locally, so your games stay yours.

Under the hood

Built from scratch, measured against the best

This project is also a piece of engineering I am proud of. The move generator is validated with perft against the standard reference positions. The classifier was benchmarked ply-by-ply against Lichess's published judgments across thousands of positions, and the accuracy model tracks the numbers the major sites report to within a couple of points on real games.

When something disagreed, I chased down which side was right and kept the receipts: the repository ships with the full benchmark suite, so every claim on this page can be re-run.

Engine: Stockfish 17.1 (GPL). Pieces: Cburnett set (CC BY-SA). Openings: the Lichess ECO dataset. Everything else: written by hand.

The scoreboard

  • Accuracy within about two points of Lichess on real games
  • Serious-error verdicts stable when re-searched four plies deeper
  • Every flagged move re-verified before it reaches you
  • Deterministic: the same game gets the same review, every run

Who made this

I am Joshua Keum. I like taking things that feel locked behind a paywall or a big company and rebuilding them from first principles to see how close one focused person can get. This is one of those projects: part chess improvement tool, part proof of what careful engineering and honest benchmarking can do. If it helps you spot the move you keep missing, it did its job.
